Appendix

A Note on the Concepts

The story uses a handful of terms that have ordinary meanings and, in Ballywobble, slightly more specific ones. None of them are new. All of them have been tried before, in different places, under different names.

Term What it means in the story Real-world analogue
The Shed The physical shed, and by extension the informal institution the village builds around it. A community group, a men's shed, a residents' association.
Ballywobble Odd Jobs The registered co-op that invoices in euros and pays tax. The outside-facing entity. A workers' co-op, a social enterprise.
The Ledger A record of time given and received, in credits that fade. Not money, not a debt. Time banking, LETS schemes, mutual credit.
The one-way valve The rule that credits cannot be exchanged for euros, or vice versa. Keeps the two systems separate. Complementary currency design; demurrage.
The Board The digital board for proposals and objections, with an annoyance gauge. A facilitation tool disguised as a joke. Consent-based decision-making; sociocracy; the "temperature check".
The Rota The list that divides work. The nearest thing the Shed has to a manager, and not a person. Self-organising teams; holacracy; the chores rota.
The Notebook Ines's design notebook: materials, energy, labour, environmental cost. Distinct from the Ledger. Life-cycle assessment; appropriate technology; the Whole Earth Catalog.
The Integral Site The online forum where plans are shared. Not named grandly, because it isn't grand. Permaculture forums; repair cafés; open-source hardware communities.

None of this is a blueprint. The story is not an argument that any of it would work everywhere, or that it should be tried everywhere. It is an argument that it worked once, in one village, for a while, and that the people involved were ordinary and the reasons were ordinary and the arithmetic was ordinary. That is all.
