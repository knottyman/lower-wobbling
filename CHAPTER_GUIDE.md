# Chapter guide — The Greenhouse at Ballywobble

This is a navigation aid for writers and AI agents. It is deliberately shorter than
`STORY.md`. The chapter files remain the actual prose; `STORY.md` remains the
authority for canon, character rules, timeline and unresolved decisions.

## The story in one paragraph

A small West Cork village starts by growing better lettuce and gradually builds
a shared shed, greenhouse, tool-lending arrangement and time-credit Ledger. The
village does not abolish ordinary money: Ballywobble Odd Jobs handles paid work
and purchases, while the Ledger records contributions inside the community.
The arrangement attracts suspicion, online misreadings and a bank risk officer
who cannot classify it. When another village asks for help, the people of
Ballywobble discover that their way of organising cannot simply be copied.
The story stays affectionate, dry and unresolved rather than turning into a
manifesto or a victory speech.

## Character appearance notes

These are the visual details agreed so far. Add further specifics only
when the author settles them; do not fill gaps with stock-character
assumptions.

- **Mabel:** 71, but her age is not the first thing people notice. She has
  short, practical grey hair, alert eyes and purposeful movements. Her
  clothes are comfortable and practical, with a little individuality.
  Her humour is dry and straight-faced; she does not signal when she is
  joking.
- **Ines:** 20. Fairly attractive in an understated, natural way. Her
  expression is observant and knowing, and she has a persuasive look that
  can make someone feel their arm has been twisted before they quite
  notice it happening. This describes her appearance and presence; it
  should not be confused with being all-knowing. Her charm is one part of
  her presence, not her whole personality. She has dark brown, naturally
  wavy, shoulder-length hair and brown eyes. Her other physical details
  remain open.

## Ines: questions before answers

Ines is often the person who notices an assumption the others have stepped
over. Let her ask concise, incisive questions that expose practical
consequences: who is responsible if the volunteer cannot come; who gets to
see delivery information; which parts of Ballywobble's arrangement does
Drumroe actually need? Her questions can be inconvenient and occasionally
annoying, but they should arise from curiosity and care rather than a need
to demonstrate that she is clever.

She is not the person with all the answers. When someone challenges her,
her first instinct is to understand why they see it differently. She
listens before deciding; she may still disagree, but sometimes what she
hears changes her mind. She can be stubborn and may occasionally feel
defensive, but she does not ask questions merely to lead people towards
a conclusion she has already chosen. Her knowing, persuasive expression
is a separate part of her presence, not evidence that she is all-knowing.

She can discover that she has made an assumption of her own and change
her mind. This keeps her a translator and systems-minded participant, not
the author's mouthpiece. Her reading and viewing may include forceful
systems critiques, but she is selective: distinguish what she encounters,
what she believes, and what she actually puts into practice. Do not turn
her into a lecturer or a stand-in for any real-world commentator.

## Continuity checks to keep visible

These are flags for the author, not permission for an agent to rewrite canon.

1. **Interlude inventory:** `STORY.md` describes five online/village moments, including the Aoife thread inside chapter 9 and a final thread after the epilogue. The manifest contains four separate interlude fragments; chapter 9 describes Aoife's thread in prose. Keep this distinction in mind when editing or moving interludes.
2. **Chapter 10/11 hand-off:** both chapters contain a closing reflection about Drumroe having different problems and scaling meaning adaptation. Check whether the repetition is intentional before removing or changing either instance.
3. **Open ending:** the second greenhouse is proposed but not resolved. Drumroe's trial is ongoing. Do not supply a neat outcome.
4. **Story bible wins:** if this guide and `STORY.md` differ, follow `STORY.md` for canon and flag the discrepancy to the author rather than silently choosing.
